Performance Marketing · Funnels

The PX Funnel System

How PX finds the bottleneck in a client's acquisition funnel, and what to do about it. One diagnosis tree, six SOPs, one handover.

The SOPs, top of funnel to bottom
Reference:

Find the choke point, then fix that

The funnel is not a set of random marketing activities. It is an acquisition system, and every system is constrained by a bottleneck. Use quantitative data to see where the bottleneck is. Use qualitative data to form a hypothesis. Then keep improving the funnel and get better results for the client.

The 11 stages

Every client funnel runs Ad, Click, Landing page, Form, Lead, Qualified, Booked, Showed, Closed. PX diagnoses it as 11 stages. Click a stage to open the SOP step that diagnoses it.

Lead volume SOP Technical QA Lead quality Booking rate Show rate Close rate
How to use this playbook
1 · Find the constraint first

Identify which stage is limiting results before you change anything. A funnel with low volume is not necessarily broken.

2 · Work top to bottom

A stage can only be judged once the stage above it is healthy or ruled out. Start at budget and move down the diagnosis tree.

3 · Establish the facts

Each SOP's first step records the numbers before diagnosing. Use consistent denominators, periods and cohorts. Formulas are on the Metrics tab.

4 · Change one thing

Pick the most plausible cause and test one change at a time. Otherwise nobody knows which change helped.

5 · Judge on the business outcome

A low CPL is not the end goal. Qualified leads, booked calls, show-ups, customers and CAC decide whether the funnel works.

6 · End with the handover

Every diagnosis ends with the same six fields. If the evidence is not there, say "inconclusive" and name what to measure. See the handover.

The checklists save as you go. Every SOP step has tickable checks. Ticks are stored in this browser only, with a done/total count and a reset link on each SOP tab.

SOP · Stages 1 to 6

Lead Volume: Not Enough Leads

Low lead volume does not automatically mean the landing page is broken. Work out whether budget, traffic cost, traffic fit, the offer or the form is limiting results, then confirm the real business outcome.

Core principle: There are three primary possibilities: insufficient budget, expensive traffic, wrong traffic, or poor conversion due to landing page tech or bad offer. The traffic can be expensive with not enough budget. The wrong people can be landing on the page. The right people can be landing, but the offer is weak. The traffic and the landing page can be incongruent, which lowers the conversion rate. Or visitors drop off in the multi-step form. Your job is to identify which one is limiting results before making changes.
Target metric
Lead conversion rate: the % of visitors who become leads by submitting your form.
Formula
(Form submissions ÷ Unique page visitors) × 100
Initial view
Last 7 days, against the previous 7 days and a 28-day view where available

Complete
Step 0Establish the facts▾

Before diagnosing anything, record:

  • Target leads per week and actual leads per week.
  • Ad spend, CPL, number of landing-page views and number of leads.
  • Campaign, ad set and ad responsible for the traffic.
  • Funnel URL and lead form or booking flow.
  • Date of the last significant campaign or funnel change.
  • Leads, qualified leads, customers and CAC for the same acquisition cohort where possible.
Use the last 7 days as an initial view. Then compare against the previous 7 days and a longer 28-day view where available. Check weekday mix, spend changes and conversion lag before drawing conclusions. For low-volume campaigns, extend the period until there is enough data for a reasonable comparison.
Do notCompare CPL from Ads Manager with leads from the CRM without checking attribution, time zones and conversion delays.
Step 1Is the budget sufficient?▾

WhereMeta Ads Manager → Campaigns → Ad sets → Columns → Customize columns

Add these columns
Amount spentResultsCost per resultImpressionsCPMLink clicksCTR (link)CPC (link)Landing page viewsFrequencyDelivery
Checklist
  • Is the campaign spending its intended daily or lifetime budget?
  • Is delivery restricted by an account spending limit, schedule, audience size, bid strategy or other delivery constraint?
  • Is the actual CPL within the acceptable target?
  • Is the current spend mathematically sufficient to generate the required volume?
Calculate

Required weekly spend = target weekly leads × acceptable CPL

Example: 100 leads per week × €30 CPL = €3,000 weekly spend.

If the campaign already generates leads at an acceptable CPL and the resulting CAC is profitable, but spend is too low to hit the volume target, the primary constraint may simply be budget.

Decision: If the budget is insufficient, calculate the required spend and confirm that the business can support it.
Do notRebuild a working funnel just because lead volume is low.

Checklist
  • Has CPM increased compared with the same campaign's previous performance?
  • Has link CTR fallen?
  • Has CPC increased?
  • Are link clicks turning into landing-page views at a similar rate to before?
  • Is one ad, placement or audience responsible for the deterioration?
Interpret the pattern
  • CPM up, CTR stable: auction costs or delivery mix may be driving higher costs.
  • CPM stable, CTR down: creative, message or audience response may be deteriorating.
  • CTR down and CPC up: investigate the creative and its relevance before changing the landing page.
  • Link clicks stable but landing-page views down: investigate page loading, redirects, tracking and accidental or low-quality clicks.

These are diagnostic clues, not proof. Compare similar campaign types, placements and periods.

Decision: Identify the metric that deteriorated first, then test the most plausible cause.
Do notChange creative, targeting and landing page simultaneously.
Step 3Is the traffic the right traffic?▾

WhereMeta Ads Manager → Ad level · GA4 → Reports → Acquisition and Landing page · CRM or lead-management system

Review each ad's
  • Hook and opening promise.
  • Offer and call to action.
  • Intended audience and actual targeting settings.
  • Landing-page destination.
  • Lead quality and downstream conversion rate.
Checklist
  • Does the ad clearly qualify who the offer is for?
  • Are the people clicking the ad within the intended market, geography and customer profile?
  • Are the leads relevant to the service being sold?
  • Are qualified-lead rate, booked-call rate and customer conversion rate holding up?
  • Do particular ads generate clicks but disproportionately poor leads?
Calculate

Qualified-lead rate = qualified leads ÷ total leads


Lead-to-customer rate = customers ÷ leads

Compare these rates by ad, campaign or source where the available volume supports a meaningful comparison.

Decision: If clicks are inexpensive but the resulting leads are consistently unqualified, investigate the ad promise, targeting, qualification and offer framing. Cheap clicks are not proof of good traffic.
Step 4Is the offer or ad-to-page message mismatched?▾

WhereGA4 → Reports → Engagement → Landing page, plus funnel analytics or session recordings

Inspect
Sessions and landing-page viewsEngagement rate and bounce rateAverage engagement timeCTA clicks and form-start eventsLead conversion rate by landing page and ad source
Checklist
  • Does the first screen repeat the same promise and outcome as the ad?
  • Is the offer immediately clear, including who it is for and what happens next?
  • Is the CTA visible without excessive scrolling?
  • Is there a credible reason to act, supported by proof, case studies or relevant details?
  • Does the page introduce unexpected costs, requirements or commitments?
  • Do visitors engage with the page but fail to click the CTA or start the form?
Interpret the pattern
  • High bounce rate and low engagement time: investigate a message mismatch, weak opening section, irrelevant traffic or slow loading.
  • Reasonable engagement but few CTA clicks: investigate the offer, page structure, trust and CTA clarity.
  • Strong CTA clicks but few form starts: investigate the transition to the form and any friction at that point.
  • Low conversion on one ad's traffic but healthy conversion on another: compare the ad promises and audience intent before rebuilding the page.

Compare equivalent traffic sources, devices and landing pages. Engagement metrics are supporting evidence, not a direct measure of offer quality.

Do notUse a universal bounce-rate or time-on-page threshold.
Step 5Where are visitors dropping out of the form?▾

WhereFunnel analytics, form platform, CRM and event tracking

For a multi-step form, record the number of unique users reaching each step:

  • Landing page viewed.
  • Main CTA clicked.
  • Form started.
  • Step 1 completed.
  • Step 2 completed.
  • Each subsequent step completed.
  • Form submitted successfully.
  • Lead received in the CRM.
Calculate each step's completion rate

Step completion rate = users reaching the next step ÷ users reaching the current step

Checklist
  • Is there a sudden drop between two consecutive steps?
  • Does the step ask for unnecessary information?
  • Are there confusing questions, validation errors or required fields that aren't obvious?
  • Does the form fail when submitted?
  • Are mobile users dropping out at a higher rate?
  • Does a successful submission produce a confirmation and a CRM record?
Decision: Fix the step with the largest substantiated drop-off. Test any change on desktop and mobile, and verify the lead reaches the CRM.
Step 6Confirm the real business outcome▾

A low CPL is not the end goal. Record:

  • Total leads.
  • Qualified leads.
  • Booked calls or appointments.
  • Show-ups.
  • Customers.
  • Revenue and gross profit.
  • CAC.
Calculate

CAC = attributable acquisition costs ÷ new customers acquired

Decision: If lead volume is low but CAC is acceptable and the funnel is profitable, increasing spend may be appropriate. If leads are plentiful but CAC is too high, investigate qualification, sales conversion and customer economics.

SOP · Stages 4, 7

Technical QA: Landing Pages and Mobile Funnels

Test the funnel the way a real visitor meets it, then check that every event and record lands where it should. Fix measurement separately from genuine conversion problems.

Use when: Lead volume suddenly drops, landing-page views don't match clicks, form completion is unexpectedly low, or mobile performance is weaker than desktop.
Technical QA checks0 / 0
Complete
AOpen the funnel as a real visitor▾
  • Open the live ad destination in an incognito or private browser.
  • Test the exact URL from the live ad, including query parameters and tracking tags.
  • Test without being logged into the website or funnel builder.
  • Follow the entire journey from landing page to successful submission.
  • Repeat on a second browser and a real mobile device.
  • Check for redirects, error pages, broken links, expired pages and unwanted pop-ups.
Fail condition: Any visitor cannot reach or complete the intended journey reliably.
BMobile layout and usability▾

Test at a narrow mobile viewport and on at least one real iOS and one Android device where available.

  • The page loads without a blank screen or layout shift that blocks the CTA.
  • Headlines and body copy are readable without zooming.
  • Buttons are visible, large enough to tap and not obstructed by sticky elements.
  • The CTA works and leads to the correct next step.
  • No horizontal scrolling, overlapping text, cut-off fields or hidden buttons.
  • Cookie banners, chat widgets and pop-ups do not cover the form or CTA.
  • The correct keyboard opens for email, phone and other input fields.
  • The form remains usable when the keyboard is open.
  • Dropdowns, calendars, file uploads and multi-step navigation work.
  • Back navigation preserves completed fields where expected.
  • Error messages are visible and explain how to fix the issue.
  • The thank-you page or success state appears after submission.
Fail condition: A mobile user cannot see, interact with or complete any part of the journey.
CLoading speed and page performance▾

ToolsGoogle PageSpeed Insights, Chrome DevTools Lighthouse and, where available, real-user Core Web Vitals

  • Run a mobile performance test on the exact landing-page URL.
  • Record Largest Contentful Paint (LCP), Interaction to Next Paint (INP) and Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS).
  • Inspect oversized images, videos, third-party scripts and unnecessary widgets.
  • Check whether the page's main headline, offer and CTA appear quickly.
  • Repeat testing if results vary significantly.
Core Web Vitals reference thresholds (75th percentile)
LCP
2.5 seconds or less
INP
200 milliseconds or less
CLS
0.1 or less

These are performance targets, not guarantees of conversion. A failed score warrants investigation. It does not by itself prove the cause of low lead volume.

Fail condition: The page is materially slow, unstable or unresponsive, especially on mobile or weaker connections.
DForm functionality▾

Run a complete test submission using an authorised test lead.

  • Every field accepts valid input.
  • Invalid email addresses and missing required fields trigger clear errors.
  • Phone validation accepts the intended countries and formats.
  • Dropdowns and conditional fields display the correct options.
  • Next and back buttons work on every step.
  • No field clears unexpectedly when moving between steps.
  • The submit button cannot be accidentally double-submitted.
  • Submission produces a visible success state.
  • The lead arrives in the correct CRM or lead-management system.
  • The lead contains the expected contact details and attribution data.
  • Any automation, notification, booking link or email follow-up triggers correctly.
Fail condition: A submission fails, loses data, produces no confirmation or fails to reach the destination system.
EPixel, events and attribution▾

ToolsMeta Events Manager → Data Sources → relevant dataset → Test Events and Diagnostics · browser developer tools · GA4 DebugView where configured

  • Confirm that the landing-page view event fires.
  • Confirm that CTA clicks and form-start events fire if implemented.
  • Submit a test lead and confirm the Lead event fires.
  • Verify that Lead fires only after a successful submission, not merely on a button click.
  • Check for missing, duplicated or incorrectly named events.
  • If Pixel and Conversions API both send the same event, check event deduplication.
  • Confirm the correct pixel or dataset and conversion event are used by the campaign.
  • Confirm UTMs persist through redirects and the form captures them where required.
  • Check that GA4 and CRM reporting record the test lead as expected.
Fail condition: The actual journey works but its conversion events are missing, duplicated or incorrectly triggered. Fix measurement separately from genuine conversion problems.
FCross-check the numbers▾

Compare the same time period:

  • Meta link clicks.
  • Meta landing-page views.
  • GA4 landing-page sessions.
  • Form starts.
  • Successful form submissions.
  • CRM leads.
Expect some gap. These systems will not always match exactly because of consent, attribution, blockers, time zones and reporting differences. Look for large or sudden discrepancies, then test the tracking and user journey before drawing conclusions.
GFinal sign-off▾
  • Desktop journey passes.
  • Mobile journey passes.
  • All form steps pass.
  • Test lead reaches the CRM.
  • Conversion event fires correctly.
  • Attribution data is captured as expected.
  • Any identified defect is documented with evidence.
  • The fix has been retested on the affected device and browser.
Handover rule: Report the exact defect, the steps to reproduce it, the affected device or browser, the evidence and the retest result. Do not report "the funnel is broken" without identifying the failure. Open the defect template →
SOP · Stage 8

Lead Quality: "The Leads Are Bad"

Establish whether there is a real quality problem, agree what a qualified lead means, find the pattern behind the disqualified leads, then decide whether the fix sits in marketing, the form or sales follow-up.

Same team: Never accept "the leads suck" as a diagnosis. Let clients take you down a rabbit hole, but always make sure that you feel, and the client feels, that you're on the same team. Marketing and sales are one team, always open to feedback and to making things better. We want the client to win, but we also need to make sure they're living in the right reality.
Lead quality checks0 / 0
Complete
Step 1Establish whether there is actually a lead-quality problem▾

Before changing targeting, ads or forms, confirm what qualifies as a good lead and inspect the actual lead data. Pull the following from the CRM or lead spreadsheet:

  • Total leads generated.
  • Contacted leads.
  • Leads successfully reached.
  • Qualified leads.
  • Disqualified leads and the reason for disqualification.
  • Appointments booked.
  • Appointments attended.
  • Sales made.
  • Revenue and CAC, where available.
Calculate
Contact rate
Leads successfully reached ÷ total leads
Qualification rate
Qualified leads ÷ total leads
Booking rate
Appointments booked ÷ total leads
Show rate
Appointments attended ÷ appointments booked (see the denominator note)
Close rate
Customers acquired ÷ qualified sales opportunities, or another explicitly agreed denominator
Cost per qualified lead
Ad spend ÷ qualified leads

Use consistent denominators and a consistent attribution window.

Checklist
  • Is the qualification rate below the agreed target?
  • Is cost per qualified lead too high?
  • Are leads genuinely disqualified, or are they simply not responding?
  • Are qualified leads failing to book, show up or buy?
  • Is the problem concentrated in a particular campaign, ad, audience, form or sales representative?
  • Is the sample large enough to support a conclusion?
Decision: If lead volume is low and qualification is low, investigate both acquisition volume and quality. If volume is high but qualification is poor, investigate whether the campaign attracts too broad an audience or needs stronger filtering.
Step 2Agree on what a qualified lead actually means▾

WhereCRM, lead spreadsheet, sales qualification criteria and client sales team

Create an agreed definition before optimising campaigns. Depending on the business, it might include:

Correct location or service areaRelevant need or problemSuitable customer or business typeBudget or ability to afford the offerAppropriate timeline and readinessEligibility for the serviceGenuine interest in the specific offer

Record each criterion as a field in the CRM or spreadsheet.

Checklist
  • Is every lead assigned a status?
  • Are disqualification reasons recorded using consistent categories?
  • Can the team distinguish "unqualified," "unreachable," "not ready," "no-show" and "lost sale"?
  • Has the client explicitly approved the definition?
  • Does the sales team use the same definition consistently?
  • Are qualification decisions supported by recorded information rather than subjective opinions?
Important: If the client says leads are poor, ask for specific examples and the reason each lead failed the agreed criteria. If the definition is wrong or incomplete, revise it collaboratively and apply the updated criteria consistently.
Do notSilently redefine a qualified lead simply to improve reporting.
Step 3Identify the pattern behind the disqualified leads▾

WhereCRM, call notes, form responses, sales recordings where permitted, and campaign or ad reporting

Review a representative sample of recent leads. Categorise each disqualified lead using a standard reason:

  1. Wrong location or customer type.
  2. No relevant need.
  3. No budget or affordability.
  4. Not ready or just researching.
  5. Misunderstood the offer.
  6. Ineligible for the service.
  7. Duplicate, spam or invalid contact details.
  8. Unreachable after the agreed follow-up process.
  9. Qualified but not converted.
  10. Other, with a documented explanation.
Checklist
  • Are leads consistently coming from the wrong industry, location or customer profile?
  • Are people saying they are just curious or not ready?

Final principle: Reminders help people remember. Scheduling makes attendance possible. A strong pre-frame reinforces why the call is worth attending. The team must diagnose which of these is failing rather than treating every missed appointment as the same problem.

Step 1Split the data before diagnosing the problem▾

1 · Attended
The prospect joined and the call took place.
2 · True no-show

SOP · Stage 11

Close Rate: Calls Happen, Sales Don't

Split every qualified opportunity into one outcome, rule out the stages above, then look at the recordings, the call structure, the lost reasons, payment options, follow-up, the handoff, pre-call content, speed and team capacity.

Draft, under review. PX drafted this SOP from a public sales training, If I Wanted To Master Sales, I'd Watch This Video (Jeremy Haynes, with guest speakers). It is being reviewed before it becomes standard. Steps 1, 2 and 12 follow the shape of the Show rate SOP. Steps 3 to 11 come from the course. Numbers quoted from the course are the speakers' claims, not PX targets.
Core principle: A low close rate does not automatically mean the closer is bad or the leads are bad. Prospects who meet the agreed criteria and attend can fail to buy for different reasons. The call isn't run well. The offer or price doesn't hold up against the alternatives. The prospect arrives with no context. The handover from the setter loses information. Or the deals that need a second conversation never get one. Most of these can be seen from outside the call, in the recordings, the CRM and the lost reasons. Find which one is limiting sales before recommending a change, and rule out the stages above it first.
Target metric
Close rate: the share of qualified sales opportunities that become customers.
Formula
Customers acquired ÷ qualified sales opportunities, or another explicitly agreed denominator
Focus here when
Lead volume, lead quality, booking rate and show rate are at or near target and sales are still short.

Step 1Establish the facts and split the outcomes▾

WhereCRM pipeline, sales call records, booking calendar and payment records

Never accept "the closer can't close" or "these leads don't buy" as a diagnosis. First check what actually happened to each opportunity.

Agree the denominator with the client and write it down. A qualified sales opportunity is usually a held sales call with a prospect who meets the agreed qualification criteria (Lead quality, Step 2). Say which one a number uses, and use the same one in every period you compare.

Categorise every qualified opportunity into one final outcome
1 · Closed on the first call
Bought on the first sales call.
2 · Closed on a follow-up
Bought on a later call or touch.
3 · Open, next step booked

4 · Open, no next step
No decision, no date, or the prospect has gone quiet.

The prospect said no, with the reason recorded.
6 · Disqualified on the call

  • Show rate: is show rate at target? A low show rate leaves too few held calls to judge close rate, and the same weak conviction that causes no-shows can carry into the call.

  • Are lead quality and show rate at or near target?
  • Is the sample large enough to support a conclusion?
Decision: If lead quality, show rate or tracking fails, diagnose that stage first. Otherwise diagnose the largest failure category: lost (Steps 3 to 6), open without next step (Step 7), or low first-call close rate (Steps 4 and 8 to 10).

Step 3Review the call recordings▾

WhereCall recording tool (Zoom, the CRM's call recording, a call intelligence tool) and the CRM notes for the same calls

The course's main instruction to managers is to review calls the way a sports team reviews game footage, and to share the best and worst calls with the whole team. A guest speaker puts it this way: "if you listen to enough sales call recordings, your leads will tell you exactly what you have to do to get more of them." The best optimizations come from won deals and lost reasons.

Pull a sample of recent calls for each closer, with won and lost calls in it. Listen for:

  • The point in the call where the prospect's interest drops.
  • The objections the prospect raises, in their words.
  • Who does most of the talking.
  • Whether the price, the next steps and a next call were stated.

Step 4Check the call structure and script adherence▾

WhereThe client's sales script or call outline, the recordings from Step 3, and the closer's own description of their process

A team can only learn why one rep closes and another doesn't if they all run the same process. Check whether a documented call structure exists, and whether the recordings follow it.

The stages the course recommends

Use them as a reference for what is missing, not a script to impose.

  • Price early. State the price or range at the start and justify it after. Price left to the end becomes a "cliffhanger" and gets unqualified prospects off the call late instead of early.
  • Ask what they need to see. "What would be the one, two, maybe three things you would absolutely need to see here today" to justify the investment. Then build the pitch around those.
  • Discovery before pitch. "A best close is a strong open": the pain, the problem it causes, and the pressure to solve it now.
  • Listen more than talk. A guest speaker's figures: top closers talk under 40% of the call, average ones about 58%.
  • Benefits, not features. Each must-see item covered as feature, advantage, benefit.
  • Raise objections before the prospect does. "A great salesman knows the objections before their lead and they beat them to it."
  • Show what happens after they buy. Onboarding, timing, what the client has to do. The same speaker reports a close rate going from 0% to 67% after adding a one to two page next-steps sheet.
  • Ask for the decision. "How soon would you like to get started?"
  • Book the next step before ending when there is no decision on the call.
Checklist
  • Is there a written call structure the whole team uses?
  • Do the recordings follow it, or does each closer improvise?
  • Is the price stated, and when?
  • Does the prospect leave the call knowing exactly what happens next?
  • Are calls ending with no decision and no next step?
Decision: If there is no shared structure, recommend the client agree one before judging individual closers. If a structure exists and one or two stages are skipped on most lost calls, those stages are the test.
Step 5Find the pattern in the lost reasons▾

WhereCRM lost reasons, call notes and the recordings from Step 3

Categorise every lost opportunity and every open-without-next-step opportunity using a standard reason:

  1. Price, or can't afford it.
  2. Needs to think about it.
  3. Needs a partner, spouse or lawyer.
  4. Comparing other providers.
  5. Timing: next month, next quarter.
  6. Trust: doesn't believe the claims, worried about the money, saw a bad review.
  7. Risk: wants a guarantee.
  8. The offer doesn't fit what they want or how they want it delivered.
  9. Other, with a documented explanation.

Calculate the share of losses in each reason, and check it by closer.

What the course says each reason points to
  • Price or can't afford it: find out whether it is cash, cash flow or not knowing about financing. Then Step 6.
  • Comparing other providers: the offer has to be visibly different at its price. A guest's example: an $80k offer against $25k alternatives fails unless it is clearly different. "You better actually be better than everybody else that can do it for them."
  • Partner, spouse or lawyer: these are scheduling problems, not objections. Get the partner on the follow-up call.
  • Timing: only real reasons to act now work. Then Step 6.
  • Trust or risk: answered with credibility assets (screenshotted testimonials, press, third-party audits, a group call with the owner), not more talking.
  • Offer doesn't fit: the difference between problem-solution fit and product-market fit. The prospect wants the outcome, but not the way it is delivered at that price.
Checklist
  • Is one reason most of the losses?
  • Is it the same across closers, or concentrated in one?
  • Does it appear before the price is stated, or after?
  • Is the client's offer priced against what prospects can see elsewhere?
Decision: If one reason dominates across all closers, it is an offer, price or trust problem. Take it to the client with the evidence, and feed it back into the ads and pre-call content. If it is concentrated in one closer, it is execution (Steps 4 and 11).
Step 6Check the payment options and the reasons to act now▾

WhereThe client's price sheet, payment processor, financing partner reports and the recordings from Step 3

  • Which payment options exist: pay in full, split pay, internal plans, third-party financing?
  • Do closers actually offer them when price comes up?
  • Is pay-in-full rewarded over split pay?The course rewards pay-in-full with bonuses, charges more for split pay, and treats financing like pay-in-full because the business is still paid in full.
  • What share of payment-plan customers stop paying?The course dropped a four-payment plan because later payments failed.
  • Is there a real reason to buy now (a price change, a cohort start date, limited places)?Urgency must be real, and the incentive must be something the prospect cares about.
  • Are closers discounting to close?The course advises against discounting. Reframe the value, or remove part of the offer for a lower price.
Decision: If "can't afford it" or "timing" is a large share of losses and the client has no financing or no real reason to act now, recommend adding one. Measure close rate and cash collected after.
Do notRecommend invented scarcity.
Step 7Audit follow-up on no-decision opportunities▾

WhereCRM tasks, pipeline stages, email and SMS logs, and calendar

"From my experience, high ticket sales typically don't happen on the very first call... They typically happen on the follow-up calls."

An opportunity with no next step is a deal the business has stopped working.

Checklist
  • Does every open opportunity have a next scheduled step and an owner?
  • Is a recap email sent after every no-decision call?
  • How many follow-up touches are made, over what period and on which channels?
  • Does each follow-up carry something useful (a case study, a short answer video, a relevant article), or is it "just checking in"?The course calls the first "value-driven follow-up".
  • Who works the leads the closers no longer have time for?The course's "cleaner" is a closer who owns those older leads.
  • Does the team ever bring open prospects together?A guest runs a private Zoom Q&A once or twice a month for everyone open in the pipeline, invited by their own rep.
Calculate

Open-without-next-step rate, and the share of all customers who closed on a follow-up.

Decision: If a large share of opportunities have no next step, fix the follow-up process before changing the call itself. Measure the follow-up close rate after the change.
Step 8Check the setter-to-closer handoff▾

WhereCRM notes, setter call recordings, booking records and the closer's prep before each call

Only applies where a setter books calls for a closer.

  • Does the closer see the form answers and the setter's notes before the call?
  • Does the setter capture the prospect's questions and tell them the closer will contact them before the call?Setters don't need every answer, but "you might have just lost a customer" if they brush questions off.
  • Does the closer contact the prospect before the call, as the setter promised?
  • Does the closer repeat questions the prospect has already answered?
  • Does what the setter promises match the ad and the offer?
  • Do set calls and directly booked calls close at different rates (Step 2)?
Decision: If set calls close materially worse than directly booked calls, or the closer arrives without the setter's notes, fix the handoff (notes, pre-call contact, consistent promise) before judging the closer.
Step 9Check what the prospect sees before the call▾

WhereThank-you page, confirmation and nurture messages, sales aid videos and any tracking of who watched them

The course treats pre-call content as part of the close. Haynes uses short answer videos as sales aids after first contact, because only 5 to 9% of visitors on the VSL pages he cites pressed play. A guest says content must take the prospect up a "buying gradient" and match the weight of the decision: content that sold a $1,000 offer produced mostly low-grade applications for a $25,000 one. Haynes also "sells the call" at booking: why it matters and what happens on it.

  • Between booking and the call, what does the prospect receive?
  • Does it answer the questions that come up on most calls (Step 3)?
  • Is the depth of the content in line with the price being asked?
  • Can the team tell which prospects engaged with it, and do they close at a different rate?

The delivery checks (does it arrive, does it load, does it work on mobile) are in Show rate, Steps 4, 5 and 9.

Decision: If prospects arrive on calls asking basic questions the content should have answered, improve the content before changing the closer. The course disagrees on whether to require it: Haynes says don't force videos, a guest reschedules anyone who hasn't watched. Measure close rate for engaged and not-engaged prospects before making it a rule.
Step 10Check speed and cadence▾

WhereCRM timestamps, call logs and SMS logs

  • How long from lead to first contact attempt?Haynes says within five minutes, and cites "a full 50%" lift.
  • How is first contact made?His routine: a text with the rep's name first, a call, and an immediate second call if the first goes to voicemail.
  • Are calls made from numbers that show as spam?His team saw "upwards of 60%" connection from local area codes, against 20 to 30% normally.
  • How long from lead to first sales call, and from first call to close?A guest treats a short sales cycle ("pipeline velocity") as a core sales metric.

Compare close rate across lead-to-call intervals (same day, next day, two to three days, four or more), the same way Show rate, Step 6 does for show rate.

Decision: If close rate falls as the lead-to-call interval grows, test faster first contact and earlier calls. Measure contact rate, close rate and sales cycle together.
Step 11Check capacity, training and how the team is managed▾

WhereCRM activity by rep, the client's sales-team structure and their management routine

PX doesn't manage the client's sales team. The evidence for these points sits in the CRM, and it belongs in the diagnosis.

  • Capacity: how many new leads, scheduled calls and follow-ups does each closer carry per day?Haynes: volume compounds, and by about week three or four a closer can no longer follow up everyone. That is the point to add setters or a cleaner.
  • Rep spread: is close rate far lower for one closer on the same leads?
  • Who sells: in-house or an outsourced closing team?Haynes says outsourced teams take only booked calls, don't follow up old leads and drop accounts that close slowly.
  • Training and review: do new hires shadow before taking calls, and does the team role-play and review calls?
  • Tracking: are each rep's calls, connection rate and close rate tracked and reviewed weekly?
Decision: If one closer is the gap on the same leads, give the client the evidence. Coaching or reassigning leads is their call. If every closer is over capacity, fix capacity before sending more leads.
Step 12Report the diagnosis and measure the fix▾

Every investigation must document:

  • Baseline close rate, first-call close rate, open-without-next-step rate and lost-reason mix.
  • The main pattern found in the data.
  • The evidence supporting the suspected cause, including the recordings reviewed.
  • The change made.
  • The review period: at least one full sales cycle.
  • The effect on close rate, cash collected, average order value and CAC where available.

Choose the most plausible corrective action first.

Do notChange the script, the payment options, the follow-up process and the pre-call content at the same time if you want to know which one helped.
Final principle: The call is where the funnel's earlier promises get tested. Recordings show what happened on the call. Lost reasons show why it didn't close. The pipeline shows whether anyone followed up. Find which of these is failing before blaming the closer or the leads.

The System at a Glance

Each stage on one line: what the symptom looks like, the first thing to check, and the SOP to open. Work top to bottom. A stage can only be judged once the stage above it is healthy or ruled out.

Stage 1Budget▾
Symptom
Volume short, CPL fine
Healthy looks like
Spend hits the daily or lifetime budget, CPL within target
First check
Is the campaign spending its intended budget, and is spend sufficient? Required weekly spend = target weekly leads × acceptable CPL.
Stage 2Traffic cost▾
Symptom
CPL up
Healthy looks like
CPM, link CTR and CPC steady against the same campaign's history
First check
CPM, link CTR, CPC and landing-page-view rate against the campaign's own history. Find the metric that deteriorated first.
Stage 3Traffic fit▾
Symptom
Cheap clicks, poor leads
Healthy looks like
Leads match the intended market; qualified-lead rate holds
First check
Qualified-lead rate and lead-to-customer rate by ad, campaign or source. Does the ad clearly qualify who the offer is for?
Stage 4Click to page▾
Symptom
Landing-page-view rate falls
Healthy looks like
Landing page views track link clicks
First check
Open the exact live ad URL as a real visitor. Check redirects, error pages and loading speed on mobile.
Stage 5Page and offer▾
Symptom
Lead conversion rate falls
Healthy looks like
Visitors engage, click the CTA, start the form
First check
Does the first screen repeat the ad's promise? Read engagement, CTA clicks and form starts together to find where visitors stop.
Stage 6Form▾
Symptom
Sudden drop between two steps
Healthy looks like
Each step keeps most of the previous step
First check
Unique users reaching each form step, and each step's completion rate. Then a full test submission.
Stage 7Tracking▾
Symptom
Meta, GA4 and CRM disagree sharply
Healthy looks like
Lead fires once, after a real submission, and reaches the CRM
First check
Submit a test lead. Confirm the Lead event fires only after a successful submission, then cross-check clicks, sessions, submissions and CRM leads for the same period.
Stage 8Lead quality▾
Symptom
"The leads suck"
Healthy looks like
Qualification rate at or above the agreed target
First check
Pull the lead data and the agreed qualified-lead definition. Is there actually a quality problem, and which disqualification reason is largest?
Stage 9Booking▾
Symptom
Lead-to-booking rate low
Healthy looks like
Qualified leads reach the calendar and book
First check
Track one cohort from form submission to confirmed booking to find the drop. Then test the booking journey as a real prospect.
Stage 10Show-up▾
Symptom
"No-show problem"
Healthy looks like
Show rate on appointments due at target
First check
Split every appointment into attended, true no-show, cancelled, rescheduled or operational failure. Diagnose the largest category.
Stage 11Close▾
Symptom
Shows but no sales
Healthy looks like
Close rate on qualified opportunities at target
First check
Split every qualified opportunity into one final outcome, agree the denominator, and rule out lead quality, show rate and tracking first.
