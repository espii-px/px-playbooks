Foundation

Pixel Seasoning

Before any campaign goes live, teach the Meta pixel what good looks like. Fire all standard events and let the data accumulate before optimizing deeper down the funnel.

Core principle: The pixel optimizes toward what it has seen. The more quality signals you feed it early, the faster and cheaper every subsequent campaign will perform. Never launch cold into a conversion objective without seasoning first.
Step 1: Launch TOFU content first
What to do
  • Launch TPF (Top of Funnel) content before any conversion campaign
  • Run all funnel events across Meta and Facebook simultaneously
  • Let the pixel observe real user behavior before you ask it to optimize
  • Do not launch prospecting until pixel has meaningful data
Why it matters
  • Meta's algorithm needs signal volume to exit the learning phase
  • Optimizing for Lead before Appointment is cheaper and faster to season
  • Each downstream event becomes more accurate as upstream events accumulate
  • Prevents wasted spend during the learning phase of key campaigns
Step 2: Fire all standard pixel events

Run these events in order of ease. Start broad and work toward the final conversion. Each event seeds the algorithm with increasingly qualified signal.

Step 3: Optimization progression
Rule: Optimize for Lead until there are enough data points to go deeper. Then progressively move the optimization event further down the funnel as each stage accumulates 50+ conversions per week.
Phase 1

Optimize for Lead

Start here. Broad signal, fastest to accumulate, cheapest to collect. Run until 50+ leads/week are firing consistently.

Phase 2

Optimize for Appointment

Move here once Lead is seasoned. The algorithm now has enough data to find people likely to book, not just submit a form.

Phase 3

Optimize for Qualified Appt

Move here when Appointment volume is strong. Now targeting the highest quality signal: vetted, sales-ready prospects.

Important warning: audience separation
Do not allow overlap: When the data calls for it, prospects being served ads should not appear in both Lead Gen and Core funnels simultaneously. Irrelevant leads should not be allowed to enter both the LG and Core Subsequent funnels. Separate exclusion audiences carefully at each transition point.

Optimization Framework

Winning Ad Multiplier

Once an ad proves itself by achieving the most spend at KPI, do not just scale the budget. Multiply it. Create 4 hook variants from the winning ad and launch them in a new CBO pack to find your next winner.

Core principle: Identify your winning ad by most spend at KPI. Then systematically create shorter and longer hook variations. Each variant tests a different entry point into the same proven message, without rebuilding the ad from scratch.
The Process
Start here: your proven winner
Identified by most spend at KPI
Winning Ad #1
Now create 4 hook variants and launch in a new CBO pack
−3 sec
Trim the hook
Remove the first 3 seconds of the ad. Find your strongest hook moment and bring it to the very start. Cut any slow preamble or setup before the real hook lands.
−7 sec
Trim deeper
Remove the first 7 seconds. This is a more aggressive cut that drops further into the content where the message is already warm. Works well when the original hook is soft.
+3 sec
Add a new hook
Add a brand new 3-second hook to the front of the existing ad. The body of the ad stays identical. Only the entry point changes. Test a different pattern interrupt or opening line.
+9 sec
Add an extended intro
Add a 9-second new intro to the front of the existing ad. Use this to add more context, agitation, or curiosity before the proven message kicks in.
Launch all 4 variants together
Launch in New CBO Pack

Each variant enters the same CBO campaign. Meta allocates budget to the best performer automatically.

Why it works
Proven body, new entry

The middle and end of the ad is already validated. It converted. You are only testing different ways to hook the viewer in. Lower risk than creating new ads from scratch.

Hook is the highest leverage point

The first 3 seconds determine whether someone keeps watching. A winner with 3 different hooks can find 3× the audience who respond to different patterns of interruption.

CBO does the work

Launching all 4 variants into a new CBO pack lets Meta's algorithm find which hook version performs best. No manual budget splitting. The winning hook self-identifies.

When to trigger this
Trigger signals
  • Ad has the highest spend of any creative in the account at or below KPI
  • Ad has been running long enough to have statistical confidence (typically 7–14 days)
  • Frequency is rising. You need fresh entry points for the same audience.
  • You want to scale the message without simply raising the budget
What to do next
  • Identify the Winning Ad #1 by most spend at KPI
  • Create all 4 variants (±3s, ±7s/9s) from the same source file
  • Launch all variants in a dedicated new CBO pack
  • Let it run for 7–14 days before making decisions
  • The new winner becomes the next input to the multiplier process

Turn the AI features off

Every one of these defaults to on. This is the phase that gets skipped, and the consequence is real: Meta rewrites copy, crops video, adds filters, and generates text over your creative. It is what makes ads look synthetic and draw "AI scam" comments.

Ad set level

Nurture does not use packs. The structure is one ad set per audience segment, with as many ads inside it as you have assets. Packs exist to keep cold tests readable, and nothing here is a test: the objective is views, and the job is coverage of a segment.

If frequency runs away: ThruPlay has no frequency cap. If the same people are seeing the ad eight times a day, the fix is not a lower budget, it is a different objective. Rebuild as Reach with a frequency cap of roughly one impression every two days. Reach trades watch time for even coverage, which is the better trade once the pool is small and the window is short.

Naming Standard

One convention across Meta and Frame.io. Anyone opening Ads Manager should immediately see what offer is being advertised, which creative pack, which concept and primary angle are being tested, which creative type, which hook, and how to find the matching file in Frame.

Locked. The Meta convention below is the current SOP and replaces every earlier version. Field order is settled: do not re-litigate it. Names become report labels and cannot be cleanly fixed later.
Meta: the three levels
1. Campaign
PX | Offer/Funnel | Stage | Objective | Launch Date
PX | GLP | PRSP | Lead | 2026 August
PX | Medical | PRSP | Lead | 2026 August
PX | Medical | NURT | Views | 2026 August
  • Launch Date is the month and year of the original launch, written out (2026 August, not 2026 AUG).
  • Never a sprint name in a campaign. Campaigns run for months.
  • Do not rename campaigns every month. The date records when it launched, not the current month.

  • Avatar and sub-niche are not in the name. Avatar stays central to creative strategy, it is just not carried in Meta naming.
  • Concept and Primary Angle sit here so winning combinations are identifiable.
  • Creative Type is Video or Image. Never mixed in one pack.

Hard rules. Do not guess a Concept or a Primary Angle if it is unclear, ask before naming. Video and Image never share a pack. No targeting descriptors such as "Broad" anywhere: broad is the default and carries no information. If a creative fits several angles, pick the primary strategic one.
Gotcha: creative names must be unique account-wide Ad set and ad names do not have to be unique, but creative objects do. Two offers running the same Concept, Angle, Type, and Pack will collide on the creative object. Prefix the offer on the creative label only. Creative names are not governed by this standard.
Frame.io: the filename

Rename every finished ad to this. Lowercase throughout, underscores between sections, no spaces. One filename, read left to right, should answer every question anyone has about an asset.

client_concept_executionstyle_angle_sprint_hook
acmeauto_pizzaoven_brollvoiceover_contrarian_augA_hook01
harbor_pizzaoven_brollvoiceover_proprietarysystem_augA_hook02
harbor_clinictour_talkinghead_proof_augA_hook01
northside_fraudfeeling_talkinghead_pain_augB_hook03
px_emptysurgeryslot_static_pain_augA_hook01
Three rules that get broken. Hooks are zero padded, hook01 and never hook1: without the zero, hook10 sorts between hook1 and hook2 in every file browser and every report. No version numbers, no v2, v3, final, FINAL2: Frame already tracks version history, and a filename that carries a version stops matching its Meta ad the moment it is revised. If you are unsure of the Concept or the Angle, leave it for the strategy lead. A guessed angle is worse than a missing one, because it silently corrupts what we think is working.
Folder structure

Inside each client offer, split the Finished Ads folder by creative type. Video and Static never mix, in Frame or in Meta.

Client Name
└── Offer            (if the client runs more than one)
    └── Finished Ads
        ├── Video
        └── Static

If a client does not use one of those formats yet, leave it out. Add it when the first asset needs it.

Client, execution style, sprint, hook
  • Client: short identifier, lowercase, no spaces. One token per client. A client running two offers gets two identifiers, e.g. acmeauto and acmeresto. A bare client token that could mean either offer is ambiguous and should not be used.
  • Execution style: talkinghead · brollvoiceover · ugc · interview · nativenews · carousel · static
  • Sprint: the production sprint, julA, julB, augA. Written Aug-A in a Meta ad name.
  • Hook: the opening variation, always two digits. hook01 · hook02 · hook03
Naming a still asset Frame uses static as the execution style. Meta's Creative Type token for the same asset is Image.

The point Someone reading a name should immediately know which client it is for, which creative concept it is, how it was produced, which strategic angle it belongs to, which sprint it came from, and which hook variation they are looking at. Nothing else needs to be opened to find that out. Once this is consistent, the same vocabulary carries into Meta, ClickUp, Canva and reporting, and we can answer which angles work across clients rather than guessing per account.
