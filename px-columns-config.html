The PX reporting view for Ads Manager. One saved preset that reads the funnel left to right, so the column you stop at is the thing that is actually broken. Build it once per ad account, then never argue about what to look at again.

One view, read in one direction

Ads Manager ships with columns built for reporting, not for deciding. This preset replaces them with a fixed order that follows the funnel: what it cost, what it produced, where people dropped, and why. You read left to right and stop at the first number that is broken. That number owns the problem, and everything to the right of it is a symptom.

Two presets, not one

The full diagnostic set is close to thirty columns wide. That is the right amount of detail for a weekly review and the wrong amount for a daily pacing check, where the horizontal scroll costs more than the data is worth. Save two.

PX | SCAN

Daily. Eleven columns, no horizontal scroll on a laptop. Answers one question: is anything on fire.

  • Off/On, Name, Budget, Amount spent
  • Results, Cost per result, Cost per appointment
  • Lead → Schedule %, Unique outbound CTR
  • CPM, Frequency
PX | DIAG

Weekly, and any time SCAN shows a problem. The full set. Answers the second question: where exactly it broke.

  • Everything in SCAN, plus the full funnel rate block
  • Creative diagnostics, thumbstop and hold
  • The whole click and landing page chain
  • Cost per click on both denominators

Ecommerce accounts get a third, PX | ECOM, which is DIAG with the lead block swapped for Purchases, Cost per purchase, Purchase ROAS and AOV. Do not carry purchase columns on a lead generation account.

Recommended changes to the current config

These are the adjustments to the config as it runs today. Each one either fixes something that reads wrong or removes something that costs screen width without paying for it.

ChangeWhy
Format Thumbstop as a percentage FixIt currently renders as a bare number, so a 24% hook rate shows as 24.03 sitting next to Hold at 16.13%. Two rates, two formats, in adjacent columns. Set it to Percentage with two decimals in the metric builder.
Add Unique link clicks AddIt is the denominator of both Click → Lead % and Click → Schedule %, and it is not on screen. Today those two ratios cannot be checked by hand against any visible column, which is how a broken custom metric survives for months.
Add Frequency, next to CPM AddA rising CPM has two very different causes: auction pressure or a burnt audience. Frequency is what tells them apart, and this is a scaling playbook. Fatigue is the failure mode that shows up first when budget goes up.
Add Leads and Cost per lead as fixed columns AddResults is relative to each campaign's optimization event, so one row reads Website Lead, the next reads Website Schedule. A mixed list cannot be sorted or summed on it honestly. Fixed event columns make rows comparable.
Put Thumbstop before Hold MoveThey are sequential moments in the same video, three seconds then the hold. Reading them out of order breaks the ladder, because a weak hold only means something once the thumbstop is healthy.
Move Purchases and Cost per purchase out OutOn a lead generation account both columns are empty on every row and sit at the far right, past two screens of scroll. They belong in PX | ECOM.
Split into SCAN and DIAG AddA daily check that requires horizontal scrolling does not get done daily. Splitting the preset is the difference between a system and a screenshot.
Do not change a custom metric's formula quietly. Every historical read of that column was made on the old definition. If a formula has to change, change it in every ad account on the same day and note the date, otherwise a month over month comparison silently compares two different metrics.

Reference

PX | DIAG, column by column

Twenty nine columns in six blocks. The order is the point: each block is a step further down the funnel, so a problem announces itself by the block it appears in. Build them in this order and do not resort the view by anything other than Amount spent.

1

Spend

Is this thing running, and is it running at the size we told it to?

#ColumnWhat it answers
1Off / OnDelivery state. First thing to check when a row shows no data at all.
2NameCampaign, ad set or ad name. The naming standard does the work here, so the name alone tells you the pack, the funnel stage and the launch date.
3BudgetWhat it was told to spend.
4Amount spentWhat it actually spent. A large gap against budget is a delivery problem, and no metric to the right of it can be trusted until that is fixed.
2

Outcome

What did the money buy, and what did each one cost?

#ColumnWhat it answers
5ResultsThe campaign's own optimization event. Useful per row, not comparable across rows that optimise for different events.
6Cost per resultThe headline efficiency number, on the campaign's own terms.
7Leads AddThe fixed lead event, the same definition on every row. This is the column you sort on.
8Cost per lead AddComparable cost per lead across the whole account, regardless of what each campaign optimises for.
9Appointments scheduledThe event that actually pays. Leads are inventory, appointments are revenue.
10Cost per appointmentThe number the client cares about. Everything else on this page exists to explain this one.
3

Funnel rates

Between the click and the booked call, which step is leaking?

#ColumnWhat it answers
11LP CONV % CustomOf the people who reached the page, how many converted. This is the landing page's score, not the ad's.
12Click → Lead % CustomClick to lead, end to end. Falls when either the ad promises the wrong thing or the page fails to deliver it.
13Click → Schedule % CustomClick all the way to a booked appointment. The truest single measure of an ad set.
14Lead → Schedule % CustomLead quality. Two ad sets at the same cost per lead can differ by half on this, and it is the difference between a good account and a bad one.
4

Creative

Did the creative stop the scroll, and did it hold?

#ColumnWhat it answers
15Thumbstop (hook) Custom MoveShare of impressions that produced a three second play. This is the first three seconds being graded, nothing else.
16Hold CustomOf the people the hook stopped, how many stayed to a ThruPlay. Read it only after the thumbstop, because holding a tiny audience is not a win.
These two split creative failure cleanly. A weak thumbstop is an opening problem, the first frame and the first line. A strong thumbstop with a weak hold is a body problem, the ad earns attention and then loses it.
5

Traffic

Did they click, did the click leave Meta, and did the page load?

#ColumnWhat it answers
17Unique clicks (all)Every click, including likes, comments, profile taps and See more. Not a traffic number. Its job is to be compared against the next column.
18Unique outbound clicksPeople who actually left Meta. This is the real traffic number.
19Unique link clicks AddThe denominator behind Click → Lead % and Click → Schedule %. On screen so the ratios can be checked by hand.
20Link clicksTotal, not unique. The denominator of LPVR.
21Landing page viewsPages that actually loaded and fired the pixel.
22LPVR CustomLanding page views over link clicks. The clearest single signal of a slow page, a broken link or a redirect eating traffic.
23Unique CTR (all)Click rate on everything, measured against reach.
24Unique outbound CTRClick rate that counts. Read this one, not the one above it.
25Cost per unique click (all)What a click of any kind costs.
26Cost per unique outbound clickWhat a visitor costs. The gap between this and the previous column is the price of engagement that never leaves the platform.
The all versus outbound gap is a diagnostic, not noise. A wide gap means people are engaging with the post instead of following it. That is usually a call to action problem or a caption that answers the question the landing page was supposed to answer.
6

Delivery

What is the auction charging, and is the audience wearing out?

#ColumnWhat it answers
27CPMCost of a thousand impressions. Moves with audience, placement, creative quality score and seasonality.
28Frequency AddAverage impressions per person. Rising alongside CPM means the audience is exhausted, not that the auction got harder.
29ImpressionsVolume. Mostly here as a sanity check on whether any rate above is readable yet.
Formulas

The seven PX custom metrics

None of these ship with Ads Manager. Build them once per ad account in the custom metric builder, with these exact names and formats, so a column means the same thing on every account PX runs.

NameFormulaFormatReads as
LP CONV %Leads / Landing page viewsPercentage, 2dpThe landing page's conversion rate.
Click → Lead %Leads / Unique link clicksPercentage, 2dpClick to lead, ad and page combined.
Click → Schedule %Appointments scheduled / Unique link clicksPercentage, 2dpClick to booked call, end to end.
Lead → Schedule %Appointments scheduled / LeadsPercentage, 2dpLead quality, in one number.
LPVRLanding page views / Link clicksPercentage, 2dpHow much traffic survives the trip to the page.
Thumbstop (hook)3-second video plays / ImpressionsPercentage, 2dp FixHow often the first three seconds stopped the scroll.
HoldThruPlays / 3-second video playsPercentage, 2dpRetention among the people the hook already stopped.
Three things about these formulas
Hold is not measured on impressions

Its denominator is three second plays, not impressions, so Hold can legitimately be higher than Thumbstop on the same row. An ad that stops 3% of people and holds 29% of them is a niche hook with a strong body, not a broken metric. If Hold were measured on impressions it could never exceed Thumbstop, and the two columns would say almost the same thing.

Two denominators, on purpose

The click ratios use unique link clicks, one per person. LPVR uses total link clicks, because a landing page view is also counted in total. Mixing the two is what produces impossible rates, so keep each formula on the denominator listed above and do not swap one in because it was closer to hand in the builder.

LPVR above 100% is expected on small numbers and is not a bug. Landing page views and link clicks are counted independently, and a single click can produce more than one page view through a refresh or a back navigation. On a row with 10 link clicks that shows as 110%. The fix is a volume gate, not a formula change: do not read LPVR under 100 link clicks.
Verifying a metric is actually right

A custom metric built on the wrong event keeps returning plausible numbers forever. Check it by hand once, on a row with real volume, before trusting it.

Lead → Schedule % = Appointments ÷ Results → 14 ÷ 18 = 77.78%

If the column and the arithmetic disagree, the metric is pointed at the wrong event, which is the usual after effect of a pixel event rename. Rebuild it, do not adjust around it.

Check the Custom tab before creating anything. If a metric with the same name already exists in the account, use it. Two metrics called LP CONV % with different formulas is worse than having none, because the column header stops telling you which one you are reading.

Decisions

The diagnostic ladder

Read left to right and stop at the first rung that is broken. That rung owns the problem. Everything to the right of it is downstream damage, and fixing downstream damage is how a week gets spent changing creative on an account with a broken landing page.

#ReadQuestionIf it is broken
1Amount spent vs BudgetIs it delivering at all?A large shortfall is a delivery problem: audience too small, bid or budget too low, or the ad set is still learning. Fix delivery first, ignore every other column on the row.
2Cost per appointmentIs it inside target?If it is, stop reading. A healthy row does not need diagnosing, and looking for a problem in one is the fastest way to break it.
3Thumbstop (hook)Did it stop the scroll?Weak against the account median means the opening fails. That is the first frame and the first line, not the offer and not the audience.
4HoldDid they stay?Good thumbstop with weak hold means the ad earns attention and then loses it. Rework the middle, keep the hook.
5Unique outbound CTRDid they click through?They watched and did not act. Usually a missing or buried call to action, or a caption that satisfies the curiosity the click was supposed to satisfy.
6All vs outbound click gapWhere did the clicks go?A wide gap means engagement that never leaves Meta: profile taps, See more, comments. Real interest pointed at the wrong target.
7LPVRDid the page load?Traffic paid for and never delivered. Check page speed, the link itself, redirects, and whether the pixel fires on the page that actually loads.
8LP CONV %Did the page convert?The ad did its job. This is a page and offer problem, and no amount of new creative fixes it.
9Lead → Schedule %Were the leads real?Volume without quality. Either the targeting is attracting the wrong person, the offer oversells, or the booking step is broken. Check the booking step before blaming the audience.
10CPM with FrequencyWhat is delivery costing, and why?CPM up with frequency up is audience exhaustion, so widen or refresh. CPM up with frequency flat is auction pressure, so that is a bid, a season or a creative quality issue rather than a fatigue one.
Volume gates

Every column in blocks 3 to 6 is a rate, and a rate on a small denominator is noise wearing a percent sign. Do not act on one until the row clears its gate.

Creative metrics

At least 1,000 impressions before reading Thumbstop or Hold.

Click and page rates

At least 100 link clicks before reading LPVR, LP CONV % or either click ratio.

Lead quality

At least 15 leads before reading Lead → Schedule %.

Benchmark against the account, not the industry. A good CPM in one vertical is a disaster in another, and a hook rate that is weak on cold prospecting can be strong on retargeting. The comparison that means something is this row against the account's own trailing 30 day median at the same funnel stage. Published benchmarks are for conversations with clients, not for decisions.
Two habits that make the view worth having
Sort on spend, diagnose on rates

Always sort by Amount spent. Sorting by cost per result floats rows with two conversions to the top and hides the row quietly spending a third of the budget. Spend tells you what matters, rates tell you what is wrong with it.

Change one rung at a time

Fix the highest broken rung, then let it run and read again. Changing creative and the page and the audience together produces a result nobody can attribute, and the next person to open the account inherits a mystery.
